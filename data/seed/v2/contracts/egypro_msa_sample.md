## 1. Interpretation (SAMPLE — FICTIONAL)

1.1 In this SAMPLE agreement "the Operator" means the fictional mobile network operator party, and "the Service Provider" means the fictional managed-services party; neither is a real entity and neither has signed anything.

1.2 "Fault" means any condition recorded by the Operator's network management system that removes or degrades service at a Site, whether or not the cause lies within the Service Provider's scope.

1.3 "Response Time" means the elapsed time from the Operator's escalation of a Fault to the Service Provider's first substantive update recorded in the Operator's ticketing system, and not the time at which the Service Provider first opened the notification.

1.4 "Restoration Time" means the elapsed time from the Operator's escalation of a Fault to the restoration of service at the affected Site, less any period properly recorded as a Stop Clock Condition under clause 6 (SAMPLE).

1.5 "Site" means a location listed in Schedule 1 of this SAMPLE agreement. No Schedule 1 exists, because this SAMPLE agreement is fiction.

## 2. Scope of Services (SAMPLE — FICTIONAL)

2.1 The Service Provider shall maintain the grid supply interface, rectifier plant, battery strings, generator sets, fuel systems, air conditioning and earthing at each Site, and shall attend Site on the Operator's escalation.

2.2 The Service Provider shall not perform work on the Operator's radio, transmission or core equipment, and shall record in the Operator's ticketing system any Fault it believes falls outside this clause 2.2 rather than attending to it.

2.3 The Service Provider shall hold at each regional store the minimum spares listed in Schedule 3 of this SAMPLE agreement. No Schedule 3 exists.

2.4 Work of a kind that would interrupt service in the normal course of business shall not be undertaken without the Operator's prior written instruction, which the Operator shall not give until it holds any approval its licence requires.

## 3. Priority Classification (SAMPLE — FICTIONAL)

3.1 The Operator classifies each Fault as Priority 1, Priority 2, Priority 3 or Priority 4 at the time of escalation, and the classification recorded at escalation governs the service levels in clause 4 (SAMPLE).

3.2 The Operator may re-classify a Fault upwards at any time, and the service levels applicable to the higher classification shall apply from the time of re-classification and not retrospectively.

3.3 The Service Provider may request a downward re-classification but shall continue to perform to the original classification until the Operator records its agreement in the ticketing system.

3.4 A Fault affecting a Site that the Operator has designated a core or aggregation Site shall be treated as no lower than Priority 2 regardless of the number of subscribers affected.

## 4. Service Levels — Response and Restoration (SAMPLE — FICTIONAL)

4.1 The Service Provider shall meet the following SAMPLE targets, measured monthly across all Faults escalated to it in the month. These figures are invented for this SAMPLE and are not the Operator's real targets.

| Priority (SAMPLE) | Response Time | Restoration Time | Update interval while open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Priority 1 | 10 minutes | 90 minutes | every 20 minutes |
| Priority 2 | 20 minutes | 150 minutes | every 45 minutes |
| Priority 3 | 45 minutes | 300 minutes | every 90 minutes |
| Priority 4 | 60 minutes | 600 minutes | every 180 minutes |

4.2 The Service Provider shall achieve the Response Time in clause 4.1 on not less than ninety-two per cent (92%) of Faults in each calendar month, and the Restoration Time on not less than ninety per cent (90%).

4.3 Where a Site is more than one hundred and twenty (120) kilometres by road from the Service Provider's nearest regional store, the Restoration Time in clause 4.1 is extended by sixty (60) minutes, and the Service Provider shall record the distance relied upon at the time it claims the extension.

4.4 The Service Provider shall maintain an availability of not less than ninety-nine point three per cent (99.3%) across all Sites in scope in each calendar month, excluding any period falling within a maintenance window the Operator has approved in writing.

4.5 A Fault closed by the Service Provider and re-reported at the same Site for the same cause within twenty-four (24) hours is one Fault, and the Response and Restoration Times run from the first escalation.

## 5. Escalation Ladder (SAMPLE — FICTIONAL)

5.1 Where a Fault is not acknowledged within the Response Time in clause 4.1, the Operator shall escalate through the following SAMPLE ladder, and the Service Provider shall make each named role contactable at every hour of every day.

5.2 Level 1 — the Service Provider's regional duty engineer, immediately on expiry of the Response Time.

5.3 Level 2 — the Service Provider's regional operations manager, thirty (30) minutes after Level 1 for a Priority 1 or Priority 2 Fault, and ninety (90) minutes after Level 1 otherwise.

5.4 Level 3 — the Service Provider's national operations director, ninety (90) minutes after Level 2 for a Priority 1 Fault, and four (4) hours after Level 2 otherwise.

5.5 Level 4 — the Service Provider's managing director, four (4) hours after Level 3, and in the case of a Priority 1 Fault the Operator may escalate to Level 4 directly.

5.6 An escalation under this clause 5 does not suspend the Restoration Time and does not of itself entitle the Service Provider to any extension.

## 6. Stop Clock Conditions (SAMPLE — FICTIONAL)

6.1 The Restoration Time is suspended for any period during which one or more of the following SAMPLE conditions applies, and for no other reason.

6.2 The Operator has asked the Service Provider in writing to suspend work, or the affected end user has asked the Operator to defer restoration.

6.3 Access to the Site has been refused, or is unavailable for a reason outside the Service Provider's control, including the absence of a landlord's representative or the closure of a controlled area.

6.4 Conditions at or on the route to the Site present a danger the Service Provider has recorded in the ticketing system, including civil disturbance, armed activity, flooding and an impassable road.

6.5 The Fault arises from the failure of the public electricity supply and the Service Provider has restored the Site to generator or battery supply, for so long as the public supply remains unavailable and the Site remains on standby supply.

6.6 The Fault lies in wiring, plant or a service that is not within the Service Provider's scope under clause 2 (SAMPLE), and the Service Provider has said so in the ticketing system.

6.7 A third-party permit or wayleave necessary to complete restoration has been applied for and is outstanding.

6.8 A stop clock is valid only where the Service Provider has recorded its start time, its end time and its reason in the Operator's ticketing system; a stop clock recorded more than sixty (60) minutes after the time claimed for its start is not valid and the period is not deducted.

6.9 The Operator may reverse a stop clock within ten (10) working days of the end of the month, stating its reason, and the Restoration Time shall be recalculated as if the stop clock had not been recorded.
